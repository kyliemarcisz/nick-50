Lisbon & Madeira · A family adventure

Nick — you've spent fifty years giving us everything. This is our turn. We're sending you (and the whole family) to one of the most beautiful countries on earth. Ten days. Two cities. The Atlantic. The very best food and wine on the planet. And memories that will outlast all of us.

I

Days 1 – 5

Lisbon

Portugal's capital sits on seven hills above the Tagus, tiled in blue azulejos and lit in that particular golden Atlantic light. It is one of Europe's oldest cities and one of its most alive. Five days here will barely scratch the surface — and you'll never want to leave.

Where to Stay

Bairro Alto Hotel

Our top pick

The finest boutique hotel in Lisbon, sitting at the edge of the bohemian Chiado neighbourhood. Rooftop bar with views across the rooftops to the Tagus. Portuguese contemporary art throughout. The service is exceptional — they remember your name after the first morning. From €350/night.

Memmo Alfama

Neighbourhood gem

Carved into the hillside of the ancient Alfama district, with a terrace and infinity pool hanging over the river. More intimate and slightly more affordable than Bairro Alto. Wake up to the view that Lisboetas have painted for centuries. From €220/night.

Verride Palácio Santa Catarina

Iconic

Only 19 suites inside an 18th-century palace on the Chiado hillside. Sweeping Tagus views, gardens, an air of total discretion. If you want to feel like a Portuguese nobleman celebrating his birthday — this is the one. From €480/night.

Where to Eat

Belcanto

✦ 2 Michelin Stars

Chef José Avillez is Portugal's most celebrated cook — and Belcanto in Chiado is his masterpiece. A 10-course tasting menu through the history and landscape of Portugal. Delicate, astonishing, unforgettable. Book months in advance and save it for a special dinner. Nick, this one is for you.

Alma

✦ 1 Michelin Star

Chef Henrique Sá Pessoa's jewel, set inside a 17th-century building in Chiado. Sophisticated without being stiff. The tasting menu marries modern technique with honest Portuguese ingredients — aged cheeses, sea urchin, bacalhau transformed into something ethereal.

Cervejaria Ramiro

Must go — no exceptions

Not Michelin-starred, but arguably the most important meal you'll eat in Portugal. Since 1956, Ramiro has served the finest Atlantic seafood in a bustling, old-school dining room. Queue before it opens. Order the garlic butter prawns, the percebes (gooseneck barnacles — look strange, taste like the ocean itself), the clams in white wine, and finish with a prego (steak sandwich). Then do it again the next night.

Tasca do Chico

30 seats · Live fado nightly

Thirty seats, a kitchen the size of a wardrobe, and the best fado dining room in Lisbon. Petiscos (Portuguese tapas), house wine, and musicians who will make the hairs on the back of your neck stand up. Book weeks ahead. This is the Lisbon you came for.

Solar dos Presuntos

Institution since 1976

A Lisbon institution run by the same family for nearly fifty years. Northern Portuguese cuisine: the presunto ham is world-class, the bacalhau with cream is perfect, and the wine list reads like a love letter to the Douro Valley. Warm, generous, quintessentially Portuguese.

What to Do

1

Morning in Alfama

Lisbon's oldest quarter, built by the Moors a thousand years ago. Wander before the tourists arrive — cobblestones, laundry drying between balconies, cats, a bakery at every corner. Climb to São Jorge Castle for the view. This is the city's soul.

2

A Day in Belém

The historical grand quarter along the river. Jerónimos Monastery (UNESCO, breathtaking). Torre de Belém. And — non-negotiable — eat a pastel de nata at Pastéis de Belém, where the recipe has been kept secret since 1837. Warm, dusted with cinnamon, next to a bica (espresso). One of the great breakfasts of the world.

3

A Day Trip to Sintra

40 minutes from Lisbon by train. One of the most magical places in Europe — a forested mountain studded with 19th-century Romantic palaces. Pena Palace (painted in deep yellow and terracotta, perched above the clouds). Quinta da Regaleira, with its spiral initiation well descending into the earth. A different world entirely.

4

An Evening of Live Fado

Fado is Lisbon's music — mournful, beautiful, born in the Alfama. Book a table at Tasca do Chico or Sr. Vinho for dinner with live performance. The singers are extraordinary. The emotion is untranslatable. This is something you carry home.

5

Sunset from the Miradouros

Lisbon is a city of viewpoints. Miradouro da Graça (the locals' favourite, never overrun). Miradouro de Santa Catarina. São Pedro de Alcântara. Each looks out over a different sea of terracotta rooftops as the light goes gold. Bring ginjinha (cherry liqueur) in your pocket.

6

Time Out Market

A beautiful 19th-century iron market hall transformed into a curated food market. All of Lisbon's best chefs have stalls — oysters, bacalhau, pastéis, craft beer, wine. Perfect for Saturday lunch. You can eat your way through the country in two hours.

II

Days 6 – 10 · 1h10min flight from Lisbon

Madeira

A volcanic island in the middle of the Atlantic — dramatic cliffs, ancient laurel forest, flowers everywhere, and the most extraordinary wine in the world. Madeira is what happens when you fly an hour southwest from Lisbon and land somewhere that feels completely unlike anywhere else on earth. Subtropical, wild, improbably beautiful.

Where to Stay

Belmond Reid's Palace

The legendary choice

Opened in 1891 on a clifftop above the Atlantic. Churchill stayed here to paint. The Empress of Austria came to recover. The gardens cascade down to private sea pools, the afternoon tea ritual is a Madeira institution, and the light off the ocean at dusk is genuinely one of the most beautiful things you will see in your life. For a 50th birthday, there is no better place on this island. From €450/night.

Choupana Hills

Forest retreat

Up in the ancient laurisilva forest above Funchal — complete privacy, no sounds except birds and wind, an infinity pool facing the Atlantic. Smaller and more intimate than Reid's, with an extraordinary spa. If you want to disconnect from the world for four days, this is where you come. From €220/night.

Casa Velha do Palheiro

Hidden estate

A 200-year-old manor house on a private estate with its own gardens, golf course, and winery. Five-star but feels like staying at a friend's extraordinary countryside estate. The restaurant serves food from the estate garden. Exceptional value for what it offers. From €280/night.

Where to Eat

William — Belmond Reid's Palace

✦ 1 Michelin Star

Even if you don't stay at Reid's, you dine here. A terrace above the Atlantic, impeccable service, and a tasting menu that reads like a portrait of the island — Madeiran goat cheese, grilled black scabbardfish, local passion fruit and honey. The wine pairing includes a flight through the great styles of Madeira wine. Book on arrival.

Armazém do Sal

Our favourite

An 18th-century salt warehouse in old Funchal, beautifully restored with exposed stone walls and candlelight. The best traditional Madeiran fine dining on the island — grilled espetada, bacalhau à lagareiro (roasted salt cod with olive oil and crushed potatoes), outstanding wine list. Warm, unhurried, perfect.

Doca do Cavacas

Essential experience

Rocky coastline west of Funchal, Atlantic waves, natural pools cut into volcanic rock — and a small restaurant where they grill lapas (limpets) directly on the rock with garlic butter. You eat them looking at the ocean. Simple, perfect, completely unforgettable. Go for lunch.

AKUA by Octopus

Best contemporary seafood

The island's finest contemporary seafood restaurant, in the modern marina. The freshest Atlantic fish, handled with intelligence. Particularly good for grilled black scabbard (the deep-sea fish unique to Madeira), clams, and the limpet risotto.

O Jango

Soul food

No tourists, all locals — the real Madeiran table. Order the espetada (whole chunks of beef on a bay laurel skewer over charcoal — the smoke and the bay leaf do something extraordinary together), bolo do caco (Madeiran garlic flatbread), and milho frito (fried cornmeal squares). Loud, warm, exceptional.

What to Do

1

Levada Walk to Caldeirão Verde

Madeira's ancient irrigation channels — levadas — cut through 25 kilometres of UNESCO-listed laurisilva forest (the last ancient laurel forest on earth). The walk to Caldeirão Verde follows a narrow ledge through tunnels and waterfalls to an impossibly green valley. You feel like the world has completely forgotten you exist. One of the great walks of Europe.

2

Sunrise at Pico do Arieiro

Drive to 1,818 metres before dawn. Watch the sun rise above a sea of clouds while the peaks of Pico Ruivo catch the first gold light. Then hike the ridge between the two highest peaks on the island — above the clouds the whole way. Surreal and breathtaking in equal measure.

3

Cabo Girão

The second highest sea cliff in Europe. A glass-floored viewing platform extends out over the 590-metre drop to the Atlantic. Stand on it. Look down. Nick — you've done scarier things.

4

The Monte Toboggan

A Madeiran tradition since the 1850s: wicker basket toboggans on runners, racing down the steep cobblestone streets from Monte village, steered by two men in white linen suits and straw hats. It is completely absurd, deeply joyful, and utterly Madeiran.

5

Porto Moniz Natural Pools

Natural lava rock pools on the wild northwestern tip of the island. Swimming in Atlantic seawater in volcanic pools, with waves crashing around you. One of those places that makes you feel genuinely alive. Drive through the north coast road to get there — spectacular the whole way.

6

Blandy's Wine Lodge

The most historic Madeira wine lodge in Funchal, operating since 1811 in a restored 17th-century convent. Tour the old lodges where the pipes are stacked floor to ceiling, then taste through all four styles — Sercial (bone dry), Verdelho (medium dry), Bual (medium rich), Malmsey (luscious sweet). These wines age for decades. Some of the bottles in the lodge are older than Portugal itself.

7

Whale Watching

Sperm whales are resident year-round off the coast of Madeira — not migratory, not seasonal. Resident. Pilot whales, bottlenose dolphins, and blue whales pass through regularly. A half-day boat from Funchal marina is one of the best wildlife experiences in Europe. In the Atlantic. On your birthday trip. Yes.

III

Eat Your Way Through Portugal

The Table

Portugal has one of the great culinary traditions in the world — simple ingredients, ancient techniques, extraordinary produce from the Atlantic, the Douro and the south. Here's what you must eat.

Pastel de Nata

The custard tart. Eat them warm from Pastéis de Belém in Lisbon — the recipe has been kept secret since 1837. Dusted with cinnamon, next to a bica (espresso). The single most important food experience in Portugal.

Bacalhau

Salt cod. The national obsession — 365 recipes, one for every day of the year. Try bacalhau à Brás (shredded with eggs and straw potatoes), bacalhau com natas (baked in cream), and bacalhau à lagareiro (whole roasted with olive oil and garlic).

Amêijoas à Bulhão Pato

Clams with garlic, white wine, butter and fresh coriander. One of the simplest and most perfect things you will eat anywhere in the world. Order them at every opportunity.

Polvo à Lagareiro

Whole roasted octopus, its tentacles crisped in olive oil, served over crushed potatoes and garlic. A revelation. Order it every time you see it on the menu — and it will be on every menu.

Percebes

Gooseneck barnacles, harvested off Atlantic rocks by men dangling on ropes. They look prehistoric. They taste exactly like the ocean. Order them at Cervejaria Ramiro and eat them with your hands.

Espetada

Madeira's great dish: large cubes of beef on a bay laurel skewer, cooked over charcoal. The smoke and the bay leaf perfume the meat in a way nothing else does. Eat it at O Jango with bolo do caco (garlic flatbread).

Lapas

Madeiran limpets. Grilled on volcanic rock with garlic butter and a squeeze of lemon. Eat them at Doca do Cavacas with your feet near the ocean. One of the most elemental food experiences on earth.

Ginjinha

Lisbon's sour cherry liqueur, served from tiny kiosks on street corners. They pour it into a small chocolate cup. You eat the cup. It costs €1.50. This is the best €1.50 you will spend in Portugal.

Poncha

Madeira's drink: sugarcane spirit, wild flower honey, lemon juice. Mixed in a wooden barrel with a wooden pestle at a poncha bar. The real version is something entirely different from what you've heard. Dangerous in the best way.

Madeira Wine

One of the world's great fortified wines, unique to the island. Four styles from bone dry (Sercial) to lusciously sweet (Malmsey). Some bottles predate the American Revolution. Taste them all at Blandy's. Buy a bottle of 20-year Bual to bring home.

Vinho Verde

Portugal's fresh young wine from the northwest — light, slightly effervescent, deceptively low alcohol. Perfect for Lisbon lunches in the sun. Try Alvarinho from the Minho region — the finest expression of the style.

Bolo de Mel

Madeira's dense, dark honey cake made with sugarcane molasses, spices, and dried fruit. A Christmas tradition eaten year-round. Break it apart with your hands — never cut it. A piece of the island to take home.

IV

10 Days · Lisbon then Madeira

The Journey

Day 1

Arrive Lisbon Check into Bairro Alto Hotel. Evening walk through Chiado — Livraria Bertrand (the world's oldest bookshop), a glass of Vinho Verde at a terrace café. Dinner at Solar dos Presuntos: presunto, petiscos, Douro red wine. Early night — the next nine days are full.

Day 2

Alfama & São Jorge Castle Wake early, walk to Alfama before 9am. Climb to São Jorge Castle. Coffee at a local pastelaria. Afternoon: Miradouro da Graça at sunset. Evening: book a table at Tasca do Chico for fado dinner. A glass of ginjinha from a street kiosk on the way home.

Day 3

Belém Day Morning at Jerónimos Monastery (arrive at opening). Pastéis de Belém next door — eat two, then eat another. Torre de Belém, Monument to the Discoveries. Lunch at Time Out Market. Afternoon free. Evening: dinner at Alma — the tasting menu, full wine pairing.

Day 4

Sintra Day Trip Train from Rossio station (40 min). Pena Palace first — arrive at opening. Walk to Quinta da Regaleira, descend the initiation well. Lunch in Sintra village. Back to Lisbon by 5pm. Dinner: Cervejaria Ramiro. Order everything. Leave nothing.

Day 5

Lisbon Free Day + Belcanto Morning at LX Factory market. Afternoon: Museu Nacional do Azulejo (the tile museum — extraordinary and often missed). Evening: Belcanto. The whole tasting menu. This is the meal of the trip. This is the meal of the year.

Day 6

Fly to Madeira Morning flight from Lisbon (1h10min). Check into Belmond Reid's Palace. Afternoon: explore Funchal old town, Mercado dos Lavradores (the covered flower and fruit market). Evening: Armazém do Sal. First poncha of the trip.

Day 7

Levada Walk Full day: Levada do Caldeirão Verde through the ancient forest. Pack lunch. Return to Funchal in late afternoon. Swim at Reid's pool. Sundowner on the terrace with a glass of Sercial Madeira watching the sun go into the Atlantic. Dinner: relaxed, at the hotel.

Day 8

Above the Clouds 4:30am alarm. Drive to Pico do Arieiro for sunrise — yes, it's worth it. Hike to Pico Ruivo. Back to Funchal by early afternoon. Monte toboggan (why not). Blandy's Wine Lodge for tastings. Evening: dinner at William. This is the dinner of Madeira.

Day 9

The Wild North Drive the north coast road to Porto Moniz — dramatic cliffs, waterfalls, cloud forests. Swim in the natural pools. Lunch: fresh grilled fish at a poolside restaurant. Stop at Cabo Girão on the way back. Dinner: AKUA in the marina. Last Madeiran wine.

Day 10

Last Morning Whale watching boat from Funchal marina (4 hours, book ahead). Return to hotel. Afternoon flight home. Nick, you are a different person from the one who landed ten days ago. Portugal does that.
